Technical Architecture Recommendation

A low-cost, low-maintenance digital foundation for Eden Cemetery
PREPARED BY 1838 BLACK METROPOLIS  ·  RECLAIM. REWRITE. & RESTORE.

The problem we are solving

Eden Cemetery carries extraordinary historical weight — documented Underground Railroad connections and roughly 93,000 burials across some 17,000 lots — including United States Colored Troops veterans and generations of Philadelphia's Black community. But the organization is operating under real financial strain.

A digital plan for Eden cannot be judged by how impressive it is. It has to be judged by one question: can Eden keep it running with almost no money and almost no dedicated technical staff? Every recommendation below is built around that constraint. The goal is a digital presence that is dignified, useful, and — most importantly — sustainable without ongoing cost or technical upkeep.

The core principle is a division of labor between two kinds of digital space:

A powerful, immersive, informative website that is low cost and low maintenance — and social interaction and events that leverage existing free social tools.

Keep the permanent, reference material on a website that requires no maintenance. Put everything that changes — events, announcements, conversation, partnerships — on free tools Eden's audience already uses. Never pay to update something you could update for free.

01A self-contained website Eden owns outright

We have built Eden a new website that is rich and interactive for the people who visit it, yet self-contained and maintenance-free for the organization that runs it. Those two things sound like they should be in tension — they aren't, and that distinction is the heart of this recommendation.

Immersive for the visitor. This is not a plain brochure page. Visitors will explore a clickable map of the grounds, search and filter the notables buried at Eden, follow the self-guided walking tours, and move through the history in interactive ways. It should feel useful, modern, and alive — a real destination, not a placeholder.

Self-contained for Eden. All of that interactivity happens in the visitor's browser. Behind the scenes, the site is a self-contained package that simply sits on Eden's existing web address and runs on its own. There is no content management system to log into, nothing to patch, and no monthly software bill. Once it is in place, it can keep working for years without anyone editing it.

What "self-contained" really means — and why it beats Squarespace or Wix. On a platform like Squarespace or Wix, every change means someone has to know how to log into that platform and edit the site — and Eden keeps paying the monthly fee for the privilege. We recommend the opposite: build everything Eden needs into the site now, then get off Squarespace entirely. No subscription, no login, no one on the hook to maintain it. The site holds Eden over comfortably for the next year — long enough to stop paying for a platform it can't currently staff.

Live content without touching the website. The one thing that genuinely changes over time is events — and those don't need a website edit at all. Eden can run its events calendar on Facebook and have it feed into the website automatically. The live content updates itself from somewhere else; the website never has to be opened up and edited to stay current.

The one recurring cost. Realistically, the only ongoing expense is the annual web hosting / domain fee Eden likely already pays. There is no separate software subscription layered on top.

What the website must make obvious. Because the site is a reference point rather than a conversation, its most important job — beyond the history and maps — is to tell every visitor, clearly and immediately, how to reach Eden and how to get involved. A visitor should never have to hunt for how to contact the organization, how to donate, or where the community is active. Wherever possible, that path should point outward to Facebook, where Eden's living presence will be.

02Facebook as Eden's living hub

Everything that does change belongs on Facebook, not the website. When Eden wants to promote an event, share news, or celebrate a discovery from the archives, the instinct is to "add it to the website." We recommend resisting that instinct every time. Updating a website costs time and often money. Posting on Facebook is free and takes minutes.

Facebook is the right home for Eden's active presence for several concrete reasons:

  • The audience is already there. Eden's core community — descendants, local residents, history and genealogy enthusiasts — is disproportionately active on Facebook. Meeting them where they already are is far more effective than asking them to visit a website.
  • Events are built in and free. Facebook's Events feature handles invitations, reminders, RSVPs, and sharing at no cost. This is the direct answer whenever Eden asks about putting events "on the site."
  • It is where partnerships form. As the brand marketing plan identifies organizations and allies for Eden to connect with, most of that relationship-building — tagging, sharing, co-promoting — happens naturally in social spaces, not on a static website.
  • It is a two-way channel. People can message, comment, and respond. The website tells people how to reach Eden; Facebook is one of the places they actually do.

Connecting the two. Events created in Facebook can be surfaced on the website by pulling them over — so the website can display Eden's upcoming events without anyone ever hand-editing the site. Facebook remains the single place Eden updates; the website simply reflects it. This keeps the "never touch the website" principle intact while still letting visitors see what's happening.

03One post, two platforms — Instagram via Meta

Because Facebook and Instagram are both owned by Meta, they are connected. Eden can cross-post from Facebook to Instagram automatically, reaching a younger and more visual audience without any extra work.

This matters for a resource-strapped organization: the walking-tour photography and archival images we are developing become content that serves both platforms from a single post. There is no separate Instagram workflow to staff — it is a setting, not a second job. Instagram is a secondary channel, not a priority: simply switch cross-posting on so Eden gets the added reach for free, while keeping all real activity centered on Facebook.

04One volunteer role: social media

The single most valuable thing Eden can add to this system is one person who owns social media — and only social media.

This does not need to be a paid staff member. We recommend Eden recruit a dedicated social media volunteer whose entire, well-defined job is to keep Facebook active: post events, share news, respond to messages and comments, and nurture the partnership connections the brand plan sets up. Narrow scope is the point. "Manage all of Eden's digital everything" is a job no volunteer will sustain. "Post to Facebook and answer messages" is a job someone can actually hold.

This role is what makes the whole architecture work, and it is deliberately the only ongoing human commitment the system requires. The website needs no one. Facebook needs one focused volunteer.

05Repurpose the contract's 50 hours to cover the site this year

Eden's current contract with 1838 includes 50 hours of writing support. Because the brand marketing plan moves Eden's ongoing communication onto Facebook, that writing time is no longer where the value is. We propose repurposing those 50 hours into website support for the coming year.

Practically, this means Eden does not have to learn to edit the site or hire anyone to do it. Whenever a small change is needed — a correction, a new photo, a tweak to the tours or notables — 1838 makes it directly, drawn from the 50 hours already in the contract. There is no new cost, and no new burden on Eden. The hours are already budgeted; we are simply pointing them at the more useful task.

This is deliberately scoped to small changes. It is not open-ended redevelopment — it is enough hands-on support to keep the site accurate and current while Eden spends this year focused on what matters most: brand awareness and getting people involved with Eden. And it carries Eden cleanly to the point where longer-term help arrives.

06A clear path to Penn Praxis next year

Within roughly a year, Eden is expected to receive support from Penn Praxis at the University of Pennsylvania. This architecture is designed to hand off to those students cleanly.

Because the website is a self-contained package, it is an ideal project for student developers to extend when that help arrives — adding features, deepening the maps, or building richer ways to explore the burial records. Nothing in the current setup locks Eden in or has to be undone first.

The sequencing is deliberate: stand up the sustainable system now, let 1838 keep it current through the 50 hours this year, and let Penn Praxis build on it next year. Eden gets a working, dignified presence immediately, at almost no cost, and a natural on-ramp for skilled help exactly when the budget and the partnership are ready for it — rather than paying for development it can't currently afford.

The shift, in one sentence

Stop treating the website as the place where Eden happens. Let the website be the immersive, self-running front door — beautiful, interactive, and maintenance-free — and let Facebook be the living room where the community gathers, events are announced, and partnerships grow. It costs almost nothing, it meets Eden's people where they already are, and it is built to survive the organization's current constraints rather than add to them.
